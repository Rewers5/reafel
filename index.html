For patients

A Holter monitor whose diary fills itself in.

No notebook, no pen, no guessing whether it is recording. A small patch on the chest records heart rhythm and movement for three days. When something feels off, the patient opens the phone app and taps once. The note lands on the same timeline the doctor reviews.

What the patient does
  • 1Wear the patch for 72 hours. It is small, waterproof and shows a steady light while it records.
  • 2Tap a note when you feel dizzy, breathless or your heart races. Two taps, no typing needed.
  • 3Carry on as usual. Walking, working and sleeping are picked up by the sensor, so you do not have to write them down.
  • 4Come to the consultation and see your own three days explained in plain language.
About the study

Built on the REAFEL data.

REAFEL, Reaching the Frail Elderly Patients for Optimizing Diagnosis of Atrial Fibrillation, collects single-lead ECG together with accelerometer data and patient-reported symptoms. reafel is the review tool on top of that data: activity-labelled ECG with patient notes, designed together with clinicians inside the diagnostic workflow.

This page and the linked prototype are a student concept from DTU course 02810. The patient data shown is synthetic. reafel is not a medical device and must not be used for diagnosis.
